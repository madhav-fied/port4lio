# Homelab dev log

### Every CS major's desire
###### 16/09/2025

Since I was a kid, I've always been fond of building and tearing apart computers and their components,
starting with my first PC that had a Pentium core, all the way to my latest one packed with an RTX 4070Ti.
Naturally, majoring in computer science got me into writing and deploying software.
Since then, the desire to run a server with the apps I actually use, fully under my own control, kept quietly growing.
Channels like NetworkChuck, LTT, and PewDiePie kept the fire burning as they were doing cool tech stuff I wanted to do myself.

___

### building the machine
##### 20/12/2025

My initial plan was to repurpose my old machine with its 8th gen i3, something cheap and quick.
But I quickly learned that virtualization doesn't play well with older CPUs, so building something new was the way to go.
I picked up an AMD Ryzen 5500X, a decent motherboard, and 16GB of RAM (not cheap). Packed it all into an Ant Esports case with a 650W PSU.
All in, I spent around ₹30,000 (~$280–300), slightly over budget. Booted up Proxmox VE and christened the machine: **Apollo**.


___

### hosting simple personal services
##### 30/1/2026

And we're live. Proxmox is running great. Got a domain and stood up a couple of services: a simple portfolio and a multiplayer blackjack platform with WebSockets for playing with friends.
Set up an Nginx LXC as a reverse proxy, went with LXC over a VM to keep things lightweight.
Hosted the app containers in their respective LXCs, configured the routes, and reserved a static local IP via DHCP reservation. Airtel's router configs are painfully restrictive by the way, ACT and Jio are a breath of fresh air.
The next challenge was linking the domain to my home IP. Getting a public static IP from the ISP is expensive and brings a lot of extra baggage. Luckily I stumbled onto Cloudflare Tunnel, which comes bundled with the domain.
The initial setup was a bit fiddly, but once I got it right the site went live. What a rewarding experience.
